How the radar works

The board asks one question: if a protocol that records no token issued one tomorrow and its record stayed as it is, what would that token be worth? It never predicts whether or when a token is issued. Every number comes from the snapshot's own figures.

The five steps

  1. Pre-token test. A protocol is pre-token when its market cap, its CoinGecko id and its symbol are all null. The page says a protocol “records no token”, never that it has none — the record is not proof.
  2. Category ratio. For each category with at least four live-token protocols, the ratio is the median of their market-cap-over-TVL ratios. Any other category falls back to the global median across all live-token protocols. Ratios are clamped to 0.05–1.00 so one outlier cannot set a category's price.
  3. Base value. TVL × the category ratio.
  4. Growth. The seven-day TVL change, clamped to −50%…+100%, moves the base by 60% of its size. Unreported growth counts as zero.
  5. Maturity and reach. A protocol that has run longer counts for more, from 0.70× for a newborn to 1.00× at three years; each extra chain up to nine adds 1%. A protocol with no listing date is treated as one year old.

What the board holds

Protocols above the floor that the watchlist leaves out are not ranked: they are arms of a larger protocol, not launches of their own. The 24 live-token protocols with the largest market caps sit apart in the Live tokens tab, at the market's own value.

Farm plans

Every row carries six steps with a cost and a time, derived from the protocol's own category, chains, TVL and age. Nothing in a plan promises a token, an airdrop or a payout — the steps are how to use the protocol like a real user, so that if it ever rewards users, the activity is there.

Implied values are this entry's own claim, from the snapshot's figures and the model above — never a price, and never a promise that a token will launch.
